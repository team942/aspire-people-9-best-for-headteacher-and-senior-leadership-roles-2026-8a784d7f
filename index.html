Senior recruitment is a different sport from classroom recruitment: fewer vacancies, longer timelines, and a process where discretion matters as much as reach. This guide compares nine agencies and platforms that actually work for headship, deputy headship, assistant headship and trust-level appointments — with honest notes on what each is best at and where it falls short. It's written for serving leaders weighing their next move, and for MATs and governing bodies building a shortlist.

What to Consider

Buyer fit: headship, deputy headship or trust-level. Not every provider that looks strong on a search page has a genuine senior desk. Check whether the agency advertises headteacher, deputy and assistant head roles as a distinct category rather than lumping them into "teaching jobs" — and whether it works with local authorities, single schools or multi-academy trusts, because a trust's hiring process is a different machine.

Core capabilities: senior-specific search and confidentiality. Leadership candidates often can't browse openly. Look for the ability to register privately, control who sees your profile, and search by phase, salary band (L1–L43 or equivalent) and contract type. On the school side, the question is whether the agency can evidence a network that includes the right governors and trust boards, not just headteachers.

Evidence and results: actual leadership placements. Vague claims about "excellence" are worthless. You want a track record in permanent senior appointments, named school partners you can verify, and consultants who can tell you which headships they filled in the last 12 months. On a job board, evidence looks different — volume of live headship adverts, and whether schools keep reposting there.

Ease of use and consultant access. A senior process usually involves a named consultant, a call, and honest feedback after interview. Ask how quickly you get a human on the phone, whether feedback is standard or only offered on request, and how the application flow works when you're in school all day. A slick interface matters less than a consultant who knows the head who is recruiting.

Price and value: who actually pays. Candidates should not pay for senior job-search support; the school pays the placement fee. Where platforms charge, it's usually for advertising or enhanced listing on the school side, plus premium candidate tools. Establish that split early, and check whether any "free" service is really a subscription with a paid tier behind it.

How We Selected

We evaluated 23 providers — national education recruitment agencies, generalist recruitment firms with education desks, and specialist job boards — and scored each against five criteria: genuine senior-level coverage (headteacher, deputy, assistant head and trust roles), permanent placement track record, transparency for candidates, quality of consultant support, and value for the school or trust paying the fee. We also spoke to the practical question every senior candidate asks: can I use this without my current employer finding out? Only nine made the list, and each one leads on something the others don't.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need an agency to apply for a headship?

No — many headships are filled through direct applications advertised by the trust or local authority. But an agency adds two things a job board can't: a consultant who puts your name in front of the right head, and feedback when a process doesn't go your way. Most senior candidates we'd advise use both.

How far ahead should I start looking?

For a September headship, senior recruitment typically runs from the autumn term through to spring, with some trusts recruiting a full year ahead. Register with agencies in the autumn term so you're in the pipeline when briefs land, and keep alerts live for the whole cycle rather than checking once.

Will my current school find out I'm looking?

Agencies handle senior candidates on a confidential basis by default, and good consultants will ask before putting your CV forward anywhere. Job boards are less discreet by nature — keep your profile private and avoid applying to your own trust without a conversation first.

What does it cost a candidate?

Nothing. Placement fees are paid by the school or trust, not the applicant. If a service asks you to pay for senior job-search support or to unlock leadership vacancies, that's a subscription product, not recruitment, and it's rarely worth the money.
